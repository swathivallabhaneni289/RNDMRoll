# [Name TBD] — daily dice-roll taste log

Combines three researched, real precedents (2026-09-10/11 session) rather than cloning any single one: BeReal's daily-ritual/prompt hook, Letterboxd's structured rating-and-log mechanic, and a dice-roll randomizer for variety and replay.

## What it is
Once a day (or on demand), a roll assigns you a category — movie, book, song, meal, place, whatever categories are stocked into the wheel. You post one entry for it: a real photo you took yourself, a title, a star rating (1-5), and an optional one-line reaction.

## Core mechanics
- Roll picks the day's category.
- Entry = your own real photo + title + rating + optional one-line note — same four fields no matter what got rolled.
- **No auto-fetched/stock images, ever** — even for movie/book/song, it's a photo you actually took (your TV screen, the book in your hand, your headphones), never a pulled-from-a-database poster or cover. This was a deliberate correction: auto-fetching official art was considered and rejected because mixing real photos with stock images breaks the personal, authentic feel that makes an Instagram-style feed work in the first place.
- Personal diary: a running, chronological log of everything you've rated — your own visual taste history.
- Friend feed: a shared board showing what everyone in your circle rolled and rated that day, with lightweight reactions.

## Why this concept
Grounded in real research, not just a vibe: BeReal proved the daily-surprise-prompt mechanic drives return visits; Letterboxd proved people enjoy rating/logging as a habit; both single-mechanic apps that lasted (vs. died, like Poparazzi/Artifact) had either a durable habit loop or a monetization fit — daily prompt + personal log combines the habit loop from one with the content structure of the other.
